## ROLE 01 — Authz & Session

### Verdict

The two-axis model is real and consistently enforced: `UserRole` gates chain stages (via `require_chain_stage` + seat ownership), `Capability` gates administrative power (via `require_capability`, checked fresh from the DB on every request, so capability revocation is session-aware within one request cycle). Row-level visibility is centralized in `scope_evaluations_for_role` (allowlist; unknown role → 403; HR excludes own subject and shielded HR-unit records; employees get an empty list unless the visibility module is on) and `_ensure_can_view` (seat-based, uniform 404 for outsiders). Production startup validators refuse insecure JWT secret, demo seed, wildcard trusted proxy, insecure CORS/PUBLIC_BASE_URL. Coverage of the checklist is essentially complete; one LOW exposure found. ## ROLE 03 — Scoring & the Legal Document

### Verdict

Score computation and the official document are correct, versioned, and tamper-evident at this commit. `compute_result` implements weighted sections with absent-section redistribution, scheme-versioned rules read from the record's stamp (`rules_for_record`), a bonus with two independent caps (scheme cap, remaining room to 100 — cap applied to the *increment* so `form + bonus = final` always holds), and half-open threshold bands with scheme validation requiring monotonic bands and a final band above 100. The final document is an immutable v6 JSON snapshot sealed at finalize time (before the status flips, inside the same transaction), rendered to PDF, hashed (sha256 stored with the bytes), and publicly verifiable through a random-token QR endpoint. Frontend preview parity is real: the frontend now implements Python's banker's rounding (`round1`) with a table-driven test, and `appliedBonus` is formula-identical to the backend. One LOW finding (verification endpoint reads mutable record fields instead of the sealed snapshot). ## ROLE 05 — Privacy & Disclosure

### Verdict

Privacy architecture is unusually well-considered and its core promises hold under adversarial reading: cohort suppression counts distinct *people* and is applied at every aggregate call site I traced; the HR-unit shield is consulted at every HR touchpoint (view, list, export, approve, claim, handover, cancel, extend, reassign, comments, PDF, notifications); `employee_evaluation_visibility` gates all three server-side read paths for subject results; self-assessment visibility is a fixed rule (`VIEWER_ROLES = {hr}`) with the scorer never notified; audit-log capability split (`view_diagnostics` vs `view_audit_log`) filters both event type and record linkage. The one systemic weakness is the **error oracle**: the project's own "404 for missing and not-yours" invariant (documented in `_not_found` and tested for two GET endpoints) does not hold on the 15+ write endpoints, where guard order leaks record existence (and for HR, shielded-record existence) through differentiated 403s. No finding rises above MEDIUM; no score, comment, evidence, or identity content exposure to an unauthorized role was demonstrated or source-proven.

### Findings

#### Top findings

# Cross-Role Findings

Defects whose combined significance emerges across roles (full entries live in their owning roles; only the combined impact is restated):

1. **The anti-enumeration invariant is enforced in code on 2 endpoints but tested on only those 2 — while 15+ endpoints violate it.** PRIV-01/PRIV-02 (Role 05, REPRODUCED) × TQ coverage structure (Role 11): `tests/test_evaluation_existence_leak.py` pins the uniform-404 contract for GET detail and summary.pdf only, so the suite is green while the invariant silently does not hold on every write endpoint. The combined lesson: the invariant needs *one* shared helper (`scoped_record_or_404`) and a cross-cutting test, not per-endpoint patches.
2. **CONC-01 (duplicate active accounts) is an integrity *and* privacy *and* audit-attribution defect at once.** Beyond the reproduced creation race (Role 07): each duplicate account can independently consume the personnel's one-time `/api/me` actions (self-assessment, acknowledgment, objection window — Role 05's "one-time" guarantees), and both accounts appear as legitimate actors in the hash-chained audit log, weakening the "who did this" evidence the chain exists to provide (Role 01/Role 06 chain integrity is unaffected — the entries are correct — but the *attribution* semantics degrade).
3. **MIG-01 (dead revocation stage) × Role 01's capability model:** the two-axis design means chain-role users holding `manage_*` capabilities retain administrative power silently after the upgrade; nothing in the UI or audit makes the retained privilege visible. The migration gate defect therefore outlives the deployment that ran it, and only a manual capability audit would reveal it.
4. **RTL-01 (bypassed Persian collation) × TQ:** `core/sorting.persian_text` has correct tests for the personnel/users surfaces it is used on, so the suite cannot detect the 5 call sites that bypass it — the same "tested where used, untested where not" shape as cross-finding 1.
5. **PERF-02 × CI environment:** the analytics row-budget test is green in CI (fresh DB, no toggled modules) and red on any real environment with module switches — meaning the query-budget regression net for analytics does not actually protect real deployments.
6. **TQ-07 × Role 03:** if the CI runner lacks pango/cairo, the entire legal-document test surface (hashing, QR, fonts, signatures) silently skips — the highest-stakes product area would ship with zero executed verification while CI stays green.
